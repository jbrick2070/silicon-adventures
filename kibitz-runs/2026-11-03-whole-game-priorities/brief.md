# Design brief for the next game pass

Choose the next improvement for someone playing from Saber's quarters through Earth, Air, Water, Fire and the return to Elron. The High Altar excursion is a second entry. The outcome is a design decision and a small testable sprint. Browser JS, CSS and Python are available. Preserve the original turn economy, native puzzles, two-colour 338 x 314 view, save slot and offline play. This is advice only. During design review read local files only. Do not launch the game, listen to cues, touch saves, edit product files or rebuild anything.

## A. First bet: give the player a trustworthy next clue during the Water journey

On the winning trace the chip changes hands more than a hundred inputs before the computer needs it. A separate documented run lost it to a skeleton. At the computer, an opt-in Where next? should distinguish a chip in the pack, a missing chip with a visible local lead, and an unknown whereabouts. Use only the current snapshot, things the player could see and original hints. Let the player decide whether to retrace steps. Keep the native command and puzzle solution behind the existing tiers. Give the control-room click a playable cue in the deepest tier. Trials with fresh, restored and bookmark snapshots must not invent a thief, recover an item by fiat, or silently spend a turn.

## B. Other candidate improvements

Compare a safer answer flow when the King, Queen or demon asks a question, a chart that lets a returning player recognize a previously visited landmark, clearer combat/retreat and save recovery, one separated foreground prop at a demonstrably misleading interaction, and a stronger return to Elron. The opening should give a new player their first trip to the King without obscuring the altar excursion. Decide which candidate has enough evidence now and which needs a baseline. Distinguish a proven failure from a usability hypothesis.

## C. Scope and decisions

First observe someone or replay a controlled scenario trying to resume at the computer with and without the chip and discover the next action with sound off. Check the stakes in all four arcs before choosing a second slice. Use a trace, synthetic snapshot or disk bookmark only for what it demonstrates. Treat a button that misleads on a rare 100-turn setback differently from an everyday annoyance. Specify the intentional cost and spoiler boundary for each action. Finish with a ranked roadmap, one buildable first slice, acceptance checks and a stop/defer decision. No new save service, map rewrite or batch of 78 room rigs.
